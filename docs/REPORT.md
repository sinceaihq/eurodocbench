# EuroDocBench: a synthetic benchmark for European invoice extraction

*Since AI technical report, version 0.1.0 (October 2026)*

## Summary

EuroDocBench is a fully synthetic, reproducible benchmark that tests whether
document-understanding systems can read European invoices and return their
key fields in canonical form. It covers 12 locales whose number, date and
currency conventions differ, five layouts, born-digital PDFs and simulated
scans. This report describes the benchmark and four baselines: a
keyword/regex extractor, a small open LLM run zero-shot on CPU, and both of
them behind open-source OCR for scanned pages.

## 1. Motivation

Public document-AI benchmarks are dominated by English receipts (SROIE,
CORD), forms (FUNSD) and US-style documents; DocILE contains real business
documents, but access requires registering for a download token, and real
documents cannot be regenerated or extended. Practitioners in Europe
need to know whether a system handles the details their documents actually
contain: decimal commas, several grouping styles, day-first dates written in
six numeric styles and in long form with inflected month names, currency
symbols before or after the amount, VAT breakdowns with several rates, and
labels in the local language. A synthetic benchmark can isolate these
details with exact labels and without privacy or licensing problems.

## 2. Design

The full specification is in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md). In short:

* **Content** — a seeded generator creates the seller and buyer (synthetic
  names built from invented syllables, real city names), 1–7 line items from
  a 14-item catalogue translated into each language, VAT at the 2026 standard
  rate (30 % of documents in countries with a reduced rate use two rates),
  dates between 2025-01-01 and 2026-09-30, optional due date (85 %), buyer VAT
  number (60 %) and IBAN (90 %). Amounts are consistent: line totals, VAT per
  rate and grand total are computed with commercial rounding.
* **Rendering** — reportlab with bundled Liberation fonts (sans, serif,
  mono) in five layouts; two label variants per field and language; long-form
  dates in 20 % and ISO currency codes in 30 % of documents; VAT numbers
  printed with spaces in 25 % and IBANs grouped by four in 70 %.
* **Scans** — the PDF rendered at 150 dpi, converted to greyscale, rotated up
  to ±1.2°, blurred, with uneven exposure, sensor noise, speckle and JPEG
  quality 55–85.
* **Safety** — VAT numbers and IBANs have the right national format but
  deliberately wrong check digits (verified against python-stdnum for all
  generated values), so they cannot belong to a real company or account.
* **Reproducibility** — every document is generated from a random generator
  seeded with its id; the published PDFs and text layers are regenerated byte
  for byte in CI.

## 3. Task and scoring

Systems return 12 fields in canonical form (ISO dates, plain decimals,
ISO 4217 codes, compact identifiers); normalisation is part of the task. The
scorer reports field accuracy, field precision/recall/F1 over non-empty
values, document accuracy (all 12 fields right), hallucinations (a value
where the document has none) and format errors, with breakdowns by locale,
layout, date style, currency style and number of VAT rates.

## 4. Baselines

| system | input | description |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword/regex rules | PDF text or OCR text | Multilingual invoice vocabulary written independently of the generator's labels (it contains many terms the generator never uses), regular expressions for European number and date formats, and arithmetic consistency (net + VAT = total). Developed on the dev split. |
| Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct | PDF text or OCR text | Zero-shot: a system prompt that describes the canonical output format, the document text (≤ 6,000 characters), JSON-schema-constrained decoding, temperature 0. Q4_K_M quantisation, served by llama.cpp (build b11730) on CPU with two parallel slots per server. No examples from the benchmark. Apache-2.0 licence. |
| EasyOCR | scan | EasyOCR 1.7.2 with its shared Latin recognition model configured for the 12 benchmark languages (Finnish uses the Swedish/Estonian character sets: EasyOCR has no Finnish pack); words grouped into lines by vertical overlap. |

## 6. Discussion

* **The text track is solvable.** The rule baseline's 100 % on the test split
  (it was developed on the dev split and on documents generated with a
  different seed) confirms that every answer is present in the text layer and
  that the labels are consistent with the documents. It also shows the limit
  of a synthetic benchmark: a system written for these templates can saturate
  it. The benchmark is therefore most informative for *general* systems —
  LLMs, vision-language models, commercial document AI — that have not been
  engineered for these documents.
* **Normalisation is where the general model fails.** On the text track the
  zero-shot Qwen2.5-1.5B finds the invoice number, names, VAT numbers, IBAN
  and currency in 94–100 % of documents, but only about half of the amounts
  and four in five dates are right. Most of these errors are not misreadings
  but incomplete conversions: of the wrong amounts, the majority are not in
  canonical form, typically half-converted — `33.261,30` becomes `33.261.30`
  (decimal comma turned into a point, grouping point kept) or keeps its
  currency symbol — and most wrong dates are copied as printed (`2.11.2025`,
  `22/09/2026`). The error table above counts each kind.
* **Arithmetic is not done.** Invoices with two VAT rates print one VAT line
  per rate and no VAT total, so `vat_amount` must be added up. The zero-shot
  model never does this: it gets `vat_amount` right on 0 of the 305 test
  invoices with two rates (65.8 % with one rate) and, in 64 % of them,
  returns one rate's amount instead. This alone explains most of the gap in
  field accuracy between one and two VAT rates (84.3 vs 78.0 %).
* **OCR noise compounds, and the two systems fail differently.** On scans,
  both pipelines reach about two thirds field accuracy and only 3–4 % of
  documents are fully correct. The rules lose fields whose label and value
  the OCR places on different lines (side-by-side header blocks), so they
  miss invoice numbers and names; the LLM reads those from context but
  still mis-normalises amounts and dates, and misread characters
  (`CZO9…` for `CZ09…`) break identifiers for both.
* **Output validity.** Despite JSON-schema-constrained decoding, 5 (text)
  and 10 (scan) of the 1,200 LLM responses were not parseable JSON and are
  scored as empty, as the protocol prescribes.

## 7. Limitations

* Single-page, template-based documents without logos, stamps, handwriting,
  multi-page tables or cross-border VAT cases (reverse charge).
* Simulated scans are milder than many real scans and have no perspective
  distortion or folds.
* Company names are synthetic and stylistically uniform.
* The baselines are small and CPU-bound by design; larger models and VLMs
  would score higher.
* LLM outputs were produced with parallel decoding slots, which is not
  bit-for-bit deterministic: a rerun can change a small number of answers.
  The published prediction files are the ones scored here.
